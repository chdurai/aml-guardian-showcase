Your first investigation.
No banking background needed.

Imagine that money moving through an account has raised a question. This workspace helps you see the transfers, inspect the reasons for concern and understand where a person needs to take over.

1. What are you trying to do?

You are playing the role of an investigator—not deciding that someone is guilty. Your goal is to answer four questions:

What happened?

Which account received or sent money? How much? Does the pattern deserve attention?

What supports that view?

Inspect the actual synthetic transfers and the guidance passages linked to findings.

What remains unknown?

A transfer pattern does not tell you the customer's intent or prove a crime. Identify unanswered questions.

Where does the software stop?

Some cases stop at a human checkpoint. You record the decision in the orange Record your decision panel near the top of the case.

Safe practice only: All four cases are fictional built-in fixtures. Do not enter real customer information. Screening is illustrative. Cases and decisions are stored in local SQLite and survive API restarts unless explicitly reset. Nothing is filed with a regulator and no payment is blocked.

What you need: A browser and the running application at http://127.0.0.1:3000/. Keep this guide in a second tab. Allow about 10–15 minutes for the first walkthrough; there is no test or time limit.

How the screen is organized: the case queue is on the left and your selected case is on the right. On a phone the queue appears above the case. The activity panel may move below the case on smaller screens. A new workspace starts empty; that is normal.

2. Follow one case from beginning to its current endpoint

1Create a practice case

  1. Click New investigation.
  2. Leave Transfer pattern selected.
  3. Click Run investigation once.

The app runs the actual Python workflow against its synthetic fixture. It may finish so quickly that you do not see a running animation. That is not an error.

You should see: account ACC-4471, a new queue row, Needs your decision, and Report consideration. The rule score should be 100/100. Creating the same fixture again creates a separate local run, even though the account is the same.

2Understand the Overview

Read What does this result mean? first. Then read Follow the money:

  • Incoming: $38,300.
  • Outgoing: $28,000.
  • Net observed flow: $10,300. This is not the bank balance; no opening balance is provided.

Expand Why this score? See the rule breakdown. The five contributions are 50 + 20 + 25 + 30 + 10 = 135, capped at 100. These are application rules, not a probability or a legal conclusion.

Ask yourself: “There are several incoming transfers and money travels out and back. What additional customer context would I need before drawing a conclusion?” Amounts below $10,000 alone do not establish intentional avoidance of reporting.

3Check the evidence

  1. Click Continue to Evidence, or Check sources in the step strip.
  2. Read the specialist findings. “Support not assessed” means the application has not checked whether a source actually proves the claim.
  3. Scroll to Observed transactions and inspect the five rows.
  4. Scroll to Retrieved guidance and click a source card.
  5. Read the exact passage in Source inspection. Click Close inspection or press Escape.

You can also click references under a finding. Alert / transactions explains the synthetic input; Screening method explains the example name check. An unavailable reference shows a warning, not a fabricated source.

You should see: three ATM deposits of $9,500, $9,700 and $9,200; $28,000 sent to SHELLCO; and $9,900 returned from SHELLCO. Guidance is a tiny in-code illustrative corpus, not live regulatory research or an independently authenticated quotation.

4Read the draft without mistaking it for a filed report

  1. Click Continue to Case draft.
  2. Read the placeholder label and the narrative.
  3. Compare its statements with the evidence you inspected.

What you see depends on how the API was started (the grey note at the top of the page says which):

  • Stub narration (default): the text starts with [stub-narrative]. No AI model is involved; it is placeholder text built from the findings.
  • Local model: a small AI model running on this computer (no internet, no cloud) writes the summary. A green box titled AI writes, code verifies lists every sentence. A tick means code checked it against the facts it cites (hover a grey F1, F2 chip to read the fact). A cross and strike-through means the sentence was excluded, with the reason, for example a number that is not in the facts, a claim that a report was filed, or calling a moderate case high-risk. Click Show the verified facts to see everything the model was allowed to use.
  • If the model is not running or keeps making mistakes, you will see Verified fact template: the facts are listed plainly instead. The case still completes.

The AI only chooses the wording. The score, recommendation and the route through the agents were fixed by code before the AI was asked, and stay the same whichever model is used.

Important: You cannot edit, save, approve or submit this draft in this release. It is read-only. Do not use it as a regulatory filing or assume its citation IDs establish factual support.

5See what ran and where it stopped

  1. Click Continue to Activity.
  2. Read the execution timeline; the full timeline shows newest entries first.
  3. Look for Human checkpoint and Human checkpoint entered.

This case paused before a human decision. A node “started” event does not mean that the human approved anything. “Step completed” means that program step returned successfully; it does not certify the correctness of its claims.

5bRecord your decision (where and how)

Where: when a case shows Needs your decision, an orange banner appears under the case header. Click Go to decision; it scrolls to the Record your decision panel, which stays visible on every tab.

  1. Choose an action. Approve SAR filing (simulated; nothing is filed), Close with no further action, or Escalate to a senior reviewer. Greyed-out options show a lock and say why code blocks them—for example, a screening HOLD must be escalated at level 1.
  2. Enter your name. It goes into the audit trail.
  3. Write a rationale of at least 20 characters that refers to the evidence.
  4. Tick the checklist confirming you reviewed the evidence and the draft, then click Submit. The line above the button tells you exactly what is still missing.

Escalation (four-eyes): the case pauses again at Level 2 · Senior four-eyes review. A different name must make the final decision; the API rejects the same reviewer.

Done when: the status becomes Workflow completed, the panel shows Human decision recorded with the decision history, and Activity ends with Workflow closure naming the reviewer.

6Return, compare and revisit

Use Previous step or the section tabs to revisit evidence. Use New investigation to try another fixture. Earlier runs and review history survive API restarts in the configured local database.

Search by account, originator or beneficiary. For example, type ACC-4471 or SHELLCO. Choose Needs your decision in the queue filter to narrow the list. Clear the search and set the filter back to All investigations if cases disappear.

The URL contains the run ID and selected tab. Refreshing or reopening that URL preserves the selected screen while that case still exists. Browser Back/Forward follows your navigation. Explicit test-data reset removes previous cases.

Invent your own story (Story studio)

The four practice stories are fixed. The Story studio lets you create new ones, and see whether the AI's guess matches what the rules actually decide.

  1. Click Open story studio (purple banner near the top) or Invent your own story on the welcome screen.
  2. Describe a story: type a situation in everyday words, for example "Money from eight different people lands in one account, then most of it leaves in a single round wire.", or click an example button. Then click Design & investigate. Expect about 5–20 seconds.
  3. Or Challenge the team: a "red-team" AI tries to move at least $25,000 without being caught. It gets up to three tries and learns from the rules that fired each time. Click Start the challenge. Expect up to a minute.
  4. The new case opens. At the top of Overview, read the purple Story studio card.

Honest limits: everything is synthetic, and nothing is filed. If the local AI is off, a simple keyword designer stands in and the card says so. A match between the AI and the rules is not a measure of detection accuracy.

Showcase: make the specialist teamwork visible

  1. Open Activity on a completed or paused case.
  2. Read Why this route? to understand the deterministic branching decision.
  3. In How the specialists worked together, select a step or use Next agent.
  4. Read Receives, Responsibility and Recorded contribution. Contributions are recorded outputs from this run, not a prewritten success story. Click a reference to inspect its source.
  5. On the last step, distinguish a completed routine closure from Paused here at the human checkpoint.
  6. Click Compare scenarios near the top of the workspace. Compare the latest actual run of each case and click Open recorded run to inspect its path. If a fixture has not been run, select Try this scenario and run it.

What this demonstrates: specialist role separation, shared findings and citations, deterministic policy routing, evidence critique, draft preparation and a human handoff. CLEAR skips the critic and human checkpoint; other recommendations visit them. HOLD preserves the screening override instead of replacing it with a transaction score.

What timing means: each completed step has an actual locally measured duration. The displayed total sums those completed steps only. It excludes waiting, orchestration and browser/network overhead. It does not measure a live model or prove time saved over manual investigation. Human waiting time is not counted as a completed step duration.

Not a live animation: the step inspector reviews recorded execution. Selecting a step does not rerun it. The graph runs sequentially with shared state; this is not a claim of parallel autonomous agents or model-selected tool use. The critic checks missing reference lists, not factual support.

Ask what a signal means: Policy assistant

You do not need to know banking terminology. Click Policy assistant beside New investigation, pick a question and click Find references.

  1. Try Does a wire above $10,000 require a CTR? A CTR concerns cash reporting; a wire amount alone does not establish a reporting obligation.
  2. Read Relevant reference extracts. Each passage has an ID and is labeled Educational summary or Demo rule note. A summary is our own learning note, not a quotation from a regulator. The background link lets you read further.
  3. Open a finished case, leave Include selected synthetic case checked, and try What guidance relates to this case? The case snapshot and related notes are shown separately. Related guidance does not prove a legal requirement applies.
  4. Expand How this answer was assembled. LangChain retrieves notes; the local AI selects IDs when available; code supplies the exact passage text. The AI cannot write a new answer or change the case.
  5. Try What is the weather in Paris? The expected result is No supported answer, not invented advice.
Honest limits: there are only seven local learning notes, not an approved bank-policy library or live regulatory feed. Without the local model, ranked extracts are explicitly labeled as extractive mode. Model errors are disclosed. Exact text provenance does not certify legal accuracy, and nothing is filed, approved or changed.

Watch this flow with ./scenarios.sh run policy-references --watch. It also checks that the case's score, recommendation and recorded activity stay unchanged.

4. Two labels answer two different questions

Status: “Where are we?”

Investigating: program steps are running.

Needs your decision: the agents paused and are waiting for you. Use Go to decision.

Workflow completed: the current graph reached its end. For Routine transfer, this is automatic closure—not human approval.

Investigation failed: execution did not produce a clean outcome. Inspect the error rather than assuming success.

Recommendation: “What deserves attention?”

No escalation recommended: rule score below 40.

Review recommended: score from 40 to 69.

Report consideration: score at least 70; not a submitted report.

Hold · resolution required: internal screening restriction; not payment blocking.

What a score does not tell you

A score of 100 means the capped rule score reached 100, or the example screening override applied. It does not mean 100% certainty of crime, model confidence, investigation completeness or regulatory compliance.

What a linked source does not tell you

A reference makes a claim easier to investigate. It does not prove that the source exists, is authoritative, matches the specific transaction or logically supports the claim. This release checks evidence links only in a limited way; its UI deliberately says Support not assessed.

5. A small glossary—without the jargon

AML
Anti-money laundering: work aimed at identifying and preventing use of the financial system to disguise illicit money.
Alert / case
An alert is a signal worth looking at. A case is the organized investigation around it. Neither is proof of wrongdoing.
Originator / beneficiary
The party sending money / the party intended to receive it. Account and party matching here is illustrative, not full identity verification.
Finding / evidence / citation
A finding is a statement produced by a step. Evidence is material you inspect to evaluate it. A citation is the link or reference connecting them.
Structuring indicator
A pattern that might warrant investigation for intentional evasion of reporting requirements. Below-threshold amounts alone do not establish the required intent.
Circular transfer / cycle
A path through which money goes out and a transfer path comes back. Context determines whether it is innocent or suspicious.
Sanctions / PEP
Sanctions restrict dealings under applicable rules. A politically exposed person (PEP) holds certain prominent public functions or related connections. The current two-name tool is not a complete service for either.
SAR
Suspicious Activity Report. “Report consideration” is only an internal recommendation in this application; no report is sent.
Agent / node / workflow
A specialist software step / a named step in the graph / the connected sequence of steps. Current specialist logic is deterministic, not autonomous model reasoning.
RAG / grounding
Retrieving relevant material to help an answer / tying its specific claims to supporting sources. Retrieval exists here; full passage-grounded generation does not.
Stub / synthetic
A placeholder implementation / fictional practice data. These let you explore the workflow without a live model or real customer information.
Human checkpoint / HITL
A pause intended for a person's input (“human in the loop”). You resume it from the decision panel; code validates the action, the reviewer and the rationale before the graph continues.
Polling / stale snapshot
The browser periodically asks the API for updates / the last saved screen may no longer be current after a connection problem.

6. If something is confusing or goes wrong

I see an empty queue.

This is expected before you create a case, after an explicit reset, or when using a different local database. Click New investigation. If a search or filter is active, clear it first.

Can the four practice cases be loaded for me?

Yes. A developer can start the API with test-data loading enabled and run npm run test-data:seed in the web folder. It runs each of the four stories through the real workflow once and replaces the current queue, so the expected results above appear without clicking Run investigation. npm run demo:slow (or demo:e2e / demo:fast) plays the whole walkthrough automatically in a visible browser, with a caption explaining each step and a Pause/Resume button. See the workbench README.

Can I watch the computer try every feature for me?

Yes. From the project folder, run ./scenarios.sh. A menu lists 14 scenarios, such as "Screening hit puts the case on hold", "Use it on a phone" and "Nothing is filed, nothing is approved". Pick one or more, choose whether to watch, and choose a speed. A browser then clicks through the workspace while a caption explains each step, and you can pause it at any time. See every scenario and what it checks.

The app says it cannot reach the API.

The browser UI and Python API are separate local services. Ask the developer to start both using the workbench README. Once they are running, click Retry or Refresh cases. Do not assume a stale case is current.

I clicked Run investigation, but it reported an error.

If the response was lost, the case may still have been created. Close the dialog, refresh the queue and look for a new run before deliberately trying again. The application does not automatically retry creation. A capacity error means wait; a 100-case session-limit error requires restarting the API, which clears all current cases.

Where is the approve or escalate button?

Open a case marked Needs your decision (the queue shows an Action needed chip, and the queue header counts them). Click Go to decision in the orange banner. If an option is greyed out, its text explains which rule blocks it. Editing the draft text is not supported.

The score is 100 but there are no transfers.

You selected Screening indicator. The example name match overrides transaction scoring. Open the score explanation and Screening method reference.

I see “Case unavailable” after opening a saved link.

The API may have restarted, or the run ID may be invalid. Choose a currently listed case or create a new one. Browser refresh does not recreate an expired case.

Why does the same account appear more than once?

Every click on Run investigation creates a separate run of the chosen fixture. The account and graph case reference can repeat; the URL's local run ID and creation time distinguish the investigations.

I see strange text or an abrupt ending in the narrative.

The stub intentionally returns a shortened prompt preview, followed by findings. That is not a production-quality generated explanation. Do not interpret this as a live AI conclusion.

With the local model, a case stays “Running” for 15–60 seconds.

That is normal on a laptop: the AI writes the summary on this computer's own processor. Every other specialist finishes in milliseconds. The first case after starting can take longer while the model loads into memory. If it never finishes, check that Ollama is running; the case will still complete using the verified fact template.

Why was one of the AI's sentences crossed out?

That is the safety check working. Code compares each sentence with the facts it cites and excludes it if it adds a number, an account balance, a stronger or weaker risk level than the score, a claim that something was filed, approved or blocked, or a word such as “cash” or “laundering” that the facts do not support. Excluded sentences never appear in the narrative itself.

What do connection labels and timestamps mean?

Connected · polling means recent requests are succeeding, not that you have a durable event stream. On a lost connection the UI marks the snapshot stale. Displayed times use your browser's local time; transaction dates belong to the fixed October 2026 fixture.

Can I use this with a real case or share the local URL remotely?

No. It is loopback-only and unauthenticated. Use fictional reviewer names: a different name demonstrates the review policy but does not verify identity or seniority. Other people's devices cannot use your 127.0.0.1 URL. Real-data use and public deployment require additional identity, privacy, audit and operational controls.

7. Your first successful trial

You have understood the current product when you can:

  1. Create Transfer pattern and identify its account and recommendation.
  2. Explain why $38,300 incoming, $28,000 outgoing and $10,300 net are not a bank balance.
  3. Open a guidance passage and explain why a linked source is not automatic proof.
  4. Recognize the stub narrative and avoid treating it as a filed SAR.
  5. Find the human checkpoint in Activity, then escalate a case and finish it as a different senior reviewer.
  6. Run Routine transfer and Screening indicator, and explain why their outcomes differ.
  7. Use the queue search, filters, tabs and Previous/Continue controls to return to a case.
Ready to try: Open the application, choose Transfer pattern and follow the four-step strip. Keep this guide beside it. No domain knowledge is assumed, and no external action is performed.
